Anthropic surface

The problem · why this exists

ZombieWells documented 100+ leaking wells across West Texas — manually.[16] The pattern (PIA request → response inbox → OCR returned records → matter folder → cross-reference against RRC filings) is the same pattern every investigative beat reporter, every environmental NGO, and every plaintiff's office in the Permian runs by hand.

Texas RRC offers a GIS Viewer and Online Research Queries but no public API for filings, NOVs, or P-13s.[17] The structured data has to be scraped. ZombieWells already does that. The product is: the codebase you've been building informally, with the UX and subscription rails on top.

And the moment is right: 29 leaking wells/day in FY26 (53% YoY surge), 11,000-well backlog, $254M federal grant + $100M state appropriation, and the RRC is openly trying to crack down on operators near zombie wells.[4][5] Demand for accountability tools is real and funded.

How it works · the flow

Subject of interest→Agency lookup→PIA request drafted (Gov Code §552) Sent + tracked→10-day clock→Response inbox→OCR RRC scraper→Wells + NOVs + P-13s→GIS overlay Matter folder→Morning brief (Claude)→Story / motion / referral

Realistic 4-week build · what ships, what's Phase 2

Week 1 TX PIA generator + agency directory + tracker. Seed list of TX state + Permian-county agencies with PIA contact emails. Claude drafts compliant §552 request. Built-in 10-business-day deadline clock per §552.221.
Week 2 Response inbox + record archiver + OCR. Dedicated reply-to per request. Inbound parser saves attachments to matter folder. Tesseract OCR. Auto-flag boilerplate denials with §552.301 appeal language.
Week 3 RRC scraper + GIS overlay. HTML scrape of RRC well-status, NOVs, P-13 filings (no API per RRC docs).[17] ZombieWells dataset overlay. Map view per matter.
Week 4 Morning brief generator + 3 paid pilots. Claude writes a daily brief in user's voice per watched topics. Pilot: you/Hawk, one Permian reporter, one environmental org.
Phase 2 NMOCD (New Mexico) + EPA Region 6 + Federal Register feeds. Adds adjacent jurisdictions and federal layer. Realistic at week 6–8, not week 4.

Why this is legally clean

Public records in, public analysis out. No PII storage, no UPL surface, no client confidentiality issues. Texas Public Information Act (Gov Code Ch 552) is a public-records regime; automating §552.221 requests is no different than selling a request template.[8] §552.108 governs what agencies can withhold — not what investigators can ask for.[9] Texas Bar Opinion 705's four obligations all apply to the lawyers using the tool, not the tool itself.[7]

The problem · sized accurately this time

Texas Office of Court Administration built a free Uniform Case Management System for counties under 20,000 population — covers Loving and possibly Ward.[19] But it only covers the court-clerk side. No DA workflows. No Marsy's Law / Article 56A victim notification engine. No Brady disclosure tracker. That gap is what Victim Voice fills.

Karpel's PROSECUTORbyKarpel and Journal Tech's eProsecutor both serve larger offices — neither names a single Texas customer publicly.[20][21] Matey AI does work for prosecutors despite the criminal-defense branding[14] — so the question for you specifically is whether Matey covers Marsy's Law notification + Brady tracking + multi-county calendar at flat-rate office pricing. I'd want your read before I'd claim it doesn't.

Why this is Phase 2 — the load-bearing question

Your exact words Thursday night: "I have rules and regulations on things like victim info and criminal histories... Not allowed to share electronically... Not allowed to store indefinitely." That constraint is real and load-bearing. I tried to verify the specific statute and could only get partway:

Texas Local Schedule PS confirms retention rules — DA case files "AV after final disposition," historical files permanent, no destruction during open litigation/PIA on pain of Class A misdemeanor under Penal Code §37.10.[10] Schedule PS does not directly address whether a 3rd-party cloud SaaS may lawfully receive, store, and transmit those records. That sits in: your office's policies, any state/county procurement rules, and Texas Gov Code §552.108(b) on victim-address confidentiality — and I won't pretend to know that without your bench. This is the question I need answered before I'd touch a single line of code on the prosecutor side.

What I can't yet prove · what I got wrong in v1

Five things I want straight before you pull a thread.

  • Matey AI — I had it wrong in v1. I called Matey "criminal-defense focused" and said it didn't compete with Victim Voice. Re-check of matey.ai says they serve both defense and prosecution. Real competitive picture depends on which Matey features you actually use day-to-day.[14]

I won't claim I know it. That's the load-bearing question for Victim Voice.
  • eFileTexas is months not weeks. OCA certification via ECF 5.0 is real and 22 vendors have done it, but the path is months, not a sprint.[22] v1's "4-week calendar sync" claim was optimistic. v2 removes it from MVP.
  • RRC public-data posture. No public API per RRC's own docs.[17] HTML scraping is the realistic path; that means the tool breaks every time they redesign a page. Not a deal-breaker but a real ops cost.
  • TX Bar Opinion 705 applies to lawyers, not the tool — but if the tool produces output a lawyer uses, the four obligations (competence, confidentiality, verification, fair billing) effectively constrain the tool's design.[7] Every framework above is designed to make Opinion 705 compliance easier, not harder.
