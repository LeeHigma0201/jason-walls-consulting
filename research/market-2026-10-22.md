# Market research for the consulting site, 2026-10-22

Produced by a workflow of 8 agents (4 researchers, 4 independent verifiers) on 2026-10-22; 396 tool calls. Each verifier opened the researcher's sources and kept only what it could confirm. **Read the verifier sections as the record; the researcher sections are proposals and keep only the items the verifier confirmed.** No search volumes exist in this file and none should be claimed. Prices are what sellers ask, not what clients paid.

### 1a. Segment detail (researcher's proposal; evidence lines above are the confirmed ones)

#### S1. Residential electrical contractors, 5-49 employees (service, panel, EV, remodel work)
- Who: Owner or office manager of a residential/light-commercial electrical shop with 5-49 employees; owner is often still in the field.
- Trigger: Owner loses a bid or permit to slow follow-up or a rejected load calc; hires first office person; renews or drops Jobber/Housecall Pro; sees a competitor with an online quote page.
- Problem: Quotes, follow-ups, invoices, job paperwork and permit-grade load calcs eat owner time; off-the-shelf field-service tools cost per seat and are used lightly.
- How they buy: Peer referral, local supply house, NECA/IBEW chapter contacts, review directories (Capterra/GetApp/Software Advice, which are paid-lead sites), vendor demos and trials. Source for directories: GetApp says vendors pay for traffic and leads (search summary; page not opened).
- Right to win: He is an IBEW Local 369 electrician who wrote an NEC Article 220 engine with tests (PORTFOLIO-EVIDENCE 1.1), sells a live product with Stripe (1.1), and ships fast with agents plus independent verification (3). He can speak the owner's language and show a calculator that cites code sections.
- Risks: Price sensitivity: EstimatorSuite's contractor survey reports median software spend of $99/month, so a $4,500 project needs a clear payback story. Fragmented buyers, long tail of tiny shops. Local trust matters, so reach is mostly Louisville/KY first. Do not claim the 220.87 method (not in his engine).
- Words they type: AI for electrical contractors, electrical estimating software, Using ChatGPT for estimating (Mike Holt thread title), best invoicing software for electrical contractors, Jobber vs Housecall Pro electrician, electrician CRM software
- Researcher scores (verifier says these are not all grounded): {'pay': 3, 'reach': 4, 'fit': 5, 'competition': 3}

#### S2. Construction-tech and trade-tech startup founders (pre-seed to Series A) who need a builder who knows the field
- Who: Founder or CTO of a seed-stage construction/trade software company whose team is engineers without field time, or a trade-background founder with no engineer.
- Trigger: Closing a pre-seed or seed round, YC demo day, a design partner contractor asks for a pilot, or an engineer-led product gets 'this isn't how we work' feedback from electricians.
- Problem: Product decisions need someone who knows NEC, panels, estimators and jobsite reality; first MVP or iOS/field app must ship before the next raise.
- How they buy: Founder networks, YC and accelerator communities, Built by Builders-type groups, investor intros, X and LinkedIn founder posts, cofounder-matching and freelance platforms. Not found: a specific survey of how they choose contract builders.
- Right to win: Rare combination in PORTFOLIO-EVIDENCE: an NEC engine in production (1.1), two App Store apps (1.1, 1.2), and a public build log of agent workflows (2, 3). He can be both domain reviewer and builder at $12,000+ MVP scope.
- Risks: Small pool; many are VC-backed with in-house engineers who may want equity, not a contractor. His public reach is mostly electrician/EV audiences, not founder audiences. Unproven: no client in this segment yet. Seller-risk: a sample of one (Bidflow) is anecdotal.
- Words they type: technical cofounder construction tech, construction tech startup MVP developer, YC construction startups, electrician startup founder
- Researcher scores (verifier says these are not all grounded): {'pay': 4, 'reach': 2, 'fit': 4, 'competition': 3}

#### S3. EV charger, solar and electrification installers (including network members and program implementers)
- Who: Owner or ops lead at a 3-50 person EV-charger, solar, or heat-pump/panel-upgrade installer, or a program implementer that coordinates many contractors.
- Trigger: A permit rejection, a surge of EV/heat pump leads, a utility rebate program launching, or a partner (OEM, dealership, utility) requiring a pre-check step.
- Problem: Pre-qualifying homes, producing permit-grade NEC load calculations, and avoiding rejected permits or wasted truck rolls for panel upgrades.
- How they buy: Manufacturer and utility installer programs, Qmerit-type networks, IBEW/NECA, EVITP training channels, trade shows, and referral from dealerships. How they pick tool vendors: not found.
- Right to win: The ChargeRight NEC engine (220.83(A) default, 220.83(B), 220.82 per PORTFOLIO 1.1), 88 test files, panel-photo vision pipeline, Stripe, and App Store iOS app are exactly a pre-qualification tool. He can white-label or adapt it, and he reads panels as an electrician.
- Risks: Permit-grade liability: output must remain a tool for a licensed electrician, not a stamp. Free calculators crowd the low end. Do not use 220.87 or 220.82 in copy (claims.json). Larger installers may already be inside Qmerit or in-house software. Evidence of buyers paying $3k-$15k for this: not found.
- Words they type: EV charger load calculator, can my panel handle an EV charger, do I need a panel upgrade for an EV charger, NEC 220.87 and upgrading equipment (Mike Holt thread title), Panel Loading Calculation (Mike Holt thread title), solar 120% rule calculator main panel upgrade, EV charger installer software
- Researcher scores (verifier says these are not all grounded): {'pay': 3, 'reach': 3, 'fit': 5, 'competition': 3}

#### S4. Home-service businesses (HVAC, plumbing, electrical) with 5-30 techs wanting AI automation
- Who: Owner of an HVAC/plumbing/electrical shop, 5-30 technicians, already on Jobber, Housecall Pro or ServiceTitan.
- Trigger: Losing calls after hours, a busy season, a new office-hire cost, or a competitor with an AI receptionist.

- Risks: Most crowded segment: AI receptionist SaaS at $25-$449/mo plus agencies. His proof is electrical, not HVAC/plumbing. No AI-receptionist product shipped. Low trust in AI is high per survey, which needs hand-holding.
- Words they type: AI receptionist for HVAC, best AI receptionist for plumbers, AI automation for home service contractors, AI for plumbers and electricians quotes, Jobber vs Housecall Pro

#### S5. NEW: Residential permit-package, electrical design and electrification-consulting firms (load-calc and plan-set producers)
- Who: Owner of a 1-20 person firm that sells panel-upgrade, heat pump, EV and solar permit drawings, load calcs and PE-stamped plan sets to contractors.
- Trigger: Order volume growth from electrification incentives, a hire to replace, or a correction-letter backlog.
- Problem: Each package needs an Article 220 load calc and drawings; reviewers reject packages on mechanical errors; margins depend on throughput.
- How they buy: Not found in my evidence. Inference: they buy tools the way small agencies do, via peers, LinkedIn and software marketplaces.
- Right to win: A tested NEC load-calc engine with editions and Annex D examples (PORTFOLIO 1.1) is the core of their process; he can offer an internal tool build at package 3 scope. An electrician reviewer is also a quality gate they lack.
- Risks: Smallest evidence base of the six; buyers may not hire outside builders; PE-stamp and liability constraints; a firm could rebuild a load-calc script cheaply. Treat as a hypothesis to test with five conversations.
- Words they type: load calculation permit what a plan checker wants, panel upgrade permit drawings, solar permit design plan set, residential load calculator NEC 220.83
- Researcher scores (verifier says these are not all grounded): {'pay': 3, 'reach': 2, 'fit': 5, 'competition': 4}

#### S6. Home inspection companies (solo to 10 inspectors)
- Who: Owner of a home inspection company; many are solo.

- Problem: Report writing time, scheduling, and judging electrical panels; software is SaaS with AI add-ons already.
- How they buy: Association channels (InterNACHI, ASHI), software marketplaces and vendor trials, peer forums. Source: not found beyond the associations' existence.

- Words they type: AI home inspection software, home inspection report software, AI report writer for home inspectors, Spectora pricing
